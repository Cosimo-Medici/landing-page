# RE landing page: message and copy audit

2026-09-30 · `/re/` · Medici & Company / Cosimo

## The decision

Lead with investor-document preparation for real estate fund managers. Name the recurring jobs immediately: LP reports, investor statements, capital calls, and distribution notices. Show a concrete sample, then invite the visitor to describe one manual task.

Current core message: **Spend less time copying figures and formatting documents. Cosimo turns property updates and investor records into draft LP reports, statements, and notices.**

The intended reader is a fund manager or operations/finance lead with a small team and recurring investor-reporting work. This is a positioning choice, not evidence that every small or midsize fund has identical needs. The copy avoids size thresholds that would exclude larger teams with the same problem.

## Criticism of the previous writing

The page looked more specific than it sounded. Phrases such as “the work comes together” required the visitor to infer the task, output, and benefit. “Helps” appeared repeatedly without explaining what Cosimo actually does. Several headlines could describe any business-software product. The CTAs described conversation vaguely and concealed whether a click opened a demonstration, booking calendar, or email.

The approved hero already named LP reporting, so it remains the visual anchor. The new supporting sentence supplies the missing mechanism and benefit immediately. Rotation expands the offer; understanding it does not require waiting for all four slides.

| Previous wording | Replacement | Why it earns its space |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The reporting work piles up. Your team shouldn't have to.” | “Stop rebuilding investor documents by hand.” | Names the manual work and removes an awkward metaphor. |
| “The work comes together.” | “From fund records to a draft LP report.” | Makes the input and output explicit. |
| “A little less assembly. A lot more room for judgment.” | “Select a source file to see what it adds to this sample report.” | Explains what visitors can do in the interactive section. |
| “Explore the workflow” | “See a sample LP report” | States what the click reveals and accurately labels the illustration. |

| “Your judgment stays in the picture.” | “Cosimo drafts. Your team approves.” | States the division of responsibility in six words. |
| “Talk through a process” | “Email us your process” | Names the action and destination. Supporting text explains the three prompts and follow-up. |

## Message hierarchy and actions

1. Audience: “For real estate fund managers.”
2. Job: named investor document in the headline, with a matching visual.
3. Benefit and mechanism: less manual document preparation; drafts assembled from fund records.
4. Immediate exploration: “See a sample LP report” scrolls to the interactive illustration.
5. Scope: four recurring document types, each explaining inputs, output, and review.
6. Responsibility: the fund team reviews and approves before sending.
7. Contact: describe one report or notice and its most time-consuming step. The email action opens a draft with three short prompts; it sends nothing automatically.

Keep the sample action fixed when the hero rotates: there is only one interactive LP-report illustration. Changing its label to match every headline would promise examples the page does not contain. The secondary hero link, “Contact our team,” goes to the contact section, where “Email our team” opens a draft email.

## Target-market evidence and its limits

[Juniper Square's investor-reporting page](https://www.junipersquare.com/learn-more/landing-investor-reporting-software) groups reporting around capital calls, distributions, performance statements, and asset summaries, and describes reducing manual work. That supports using familiar document names instead of broad automation language.

[Carta's discussion of fund audit trails](https://carta.com/learn/private-funds/management/fund-audits/audit-trail/) describes private-market information spread across formats and sources and the importance of reliable records. That supports naming source records and review responsibilities. It does not establish that Cosimo supplies an audit trail.

These are vendor sources describing category problems and terminology. They do not prove conversion performance, quantify demand in our segment, or validate Medici's implementation. The copy is a reasoned hypothesis to test with real prospects, not a claim of completed customer research.

## Claim boundaries

- The user-provided Ardsley workflow list is the product-scope input; workflows remain under development/testing. This marketing pass does not certify their production readiness.
- The interactive scene and documents are explicitly illustrative and contain fictional sample data. They are not actual customer results or a recorded product run. The real demo remains separate work under EXE-45.
- No invented hours saved, accuracy percentages, customer counts, testimonials, integration coverage, or implementation-speed promises.
- Investor notices use approved allocations; the copy does not conflate drafting notices with executing payments or independently approving fund calculations.
- Tax delivery, onboarding, and waterfall calculations stay outside this page's four featured examples. This keeps the initial message focused; it does not remove them from the wider product roadmap.
- The human-review language describes the intended workflow, not a security certification or proof of an enforced approval control.

## Remaining weakness to validate

Clarity alone is not differentiation. Established vendors also talk about reporting and less manual work. The next material improvement is a real input-to-output demonstration on representative fund records, showing exactly what Cosimo prepares and where the team intervenes. Do not fill that proof gap with stronger adjectives.

For an early comprehension check, show the hero briefly to prospects and ask: who is this for, what does it prepare, what work does it reduce, and what happens when you click? Record their unaided answers. The “two-second” goal is a writing constraint; it has not been user-tested.


## Applied follow-up: direct language throughout

The second implementation pass replaces the remaining broad wording in the live page source, including the JavaScript-generated captions:

- Lead with the manual work reduced: copying figures and formatting documents. Follow immediately with the source records and draft outputs.
- Change the pain headline to “Stop copying figures into investor reports every quarter.” The supporting copy describes the sequence the fund team actually repeats.
- Replace “Four recurring jobs” with “LP reports, statements, and investor notices.” Visitors can identify the scope from the heading alone.
- Explain each document's inputs and output in the workflow descriptions. Keep the approval responsibility in its dedicated section instead of repeating it at the end of every description.
- Explain the interactive action as selecting a file to highlight the figures or commentary it supplies. Keep static and dynamic copy consistent.
- Ask “What are you still preparing by hand?” and specify the three useful details for an initial conversation. “Email our team” makes the actual contact mechanism explicit; the draft requires only a few sentences.
- Preserve the approved rotating headline and document motion. The real-demo proof gap remains open; copy edits cannot resolve it.

Validation: static build, JavaScript syntax, and whitespace checks; desktop and mobile inspection of the changed text and contact action. No new product capability claims or numerical savings promises.
