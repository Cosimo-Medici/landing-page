# Deep hedge fund buyer audit

7 October 2026. Independent review as a skeptical COO/CFO/IR lead at a small or midsize hedge fund. Source snapshot: `src/hedge/index.html`, `src/js/hedge-report.js`, shared `src/js/re.js` and `src/css/re.css`, and `docs/hedge-research.md`. No prior audit conclusions were read. No source files were edited.

## Buyer verdict

The page is coherent, attractive, and more financially careful than its simple demonstration initially suggests. I understand that Cosimo prepares drafts after my administrator produces the numbers. I would inspect the sample. I do not yet see enough incremental work being removed to prioritize a sales conversation over asking my administrator, operations analyst, or existing DDQ provider to do this.

The strongest proposed job is **assembling the manager's review package across administrator files, PM commentary and policy evidence, while leaving unresolved questions visible**. The current sample instead begins with files that already contain the consolidated data and the conclusions. It proves presentation quality and arithmetic more convincingly than it proves operational usefulness. This is fixable with an honest authored example; a real product video is not the only route.

Evidence ratings below: **A** = directly visible/source-verifiable; **B** = primary industry/vendor evidence supports the buyer objection; **C** = design or conversion judgment requiring buyer validation. Priorities are relative implementation order, not legal severity.

## Buyer context and alternatives: four primary sources

1. **The lean-team problem is real.** AIMA/Marex's September 2024 emerging manager research covers firms managing up to $500m and reports pressure to run lean operations alongside higher investor expectations for communication and diligence. It supports this audience and problem, not an invented savings percentage or a current universal benchmark. [AIMA release](https://www.aima.org/article/press-release-how-are-emerging-hedge-fund-managers-attracting-capital-and-keeping-their-edge.html).
2. **The administrator is already a substantial alternative.** SS&C explicitly offers hedge fund NAV calculation, investor reporting, reconciliation, investor services and subscriptions/redemptions capabilities. This is vendor positioning, not an independent product evaluation. The site's wedge must explain what remains with the manager after these services, rather than imply the administrator only emails raw numbers. [SS&C hedge fund services](https://www.ssctech.com/industry/hedge-fund).
3. **Sourced DDQ drafting is an established category.** DiligenceVault's September 2024 vendor case study describes a large hedge fund replacing version-fragmented Word DDQs with collaboration and pre-approved responses. The case is a larger fund, so it does not prove economic fit for the target customer. It does show that keeping approved answers current is not a distinctive claim by itself. [DiligenceVault case study](https://diligencevault.com/digitizing-standard-ddq-for-a-10-15bn-hedge-fund-manager/).
4. **Manager-generated summaries do not substitute for independent verification.** SBAI's September 2017 Administrator Transparency Reporting resource describes independent verification of NAV, pricing sources, assets/liabilities and counterparties. This is durable diligence context, not current law. Retain the page's distinction between a review workpaper and assurance. [SBAI Administrator Transparency Reporting](https://www.sbai.org/resource/administrator-transparency-reporting-september.html).

Excel already performs the sample arithmetic. A generic document assistant can plausibly rewrite three approved paragraphs; that is a buyer objection, not a tested comparative claim. A dedicated DDQ system already competes on approved answers. The useful question is therefore: **What cross-file review does my fund team stop repeating, and how can I check the resulting draft?**

## Every section: what earns attention and what loses it

**Navigation and first five seconds.** Desktop and mobile immediately identify the hedge sector and lead with a recognizable deliverable. The serif, purple, paper stack and typewriter are a coherent brand; preserve them. “Investor letters” is understandable, but could describe a copywriting tool. The explanatory paragraph does the real positioning work. A stronger stable lede would be: “Turn administrator-approved returns and PM notes into an investor draft, with close questions and missing DDQ evidence laid out for your team.” This is proposed workflow language, not a newly verified capability.

The “Before your morning coffee” promise is memorable but broad. The shared promise rotation also includes “In a jiffy” and “On your desk today” (`re.js:31`); it contains no hedge-specific scope or measured evidence. Preserve the typewriter, and keep draft/review language immediately adjacent. Do not compound the playful promise with specific time-saving or turnaround claims unless measured. The $125,000 open item in the document stack communicates more operational substance than the decorative chart.

**Intro.** “The month has closed. Your inbox hasn't” is the best audience-specific framing on the page. It correctly names the handoff between administrator, PM and allocator. Keep it, but connect it directly to the proof instead of spending another large vertical section restating the hero. “Your administrator produces the fund records; your team still assembles commentary, review questions and investor responses” is the intended boundary. Do not assert that all administrators omit those services.

**Source/output workbench.** The dark-to-paper contrast makes input and output instantly distinguishable. Three examples cover writing, exception review and evidence reuse. That is a good scope. The weakness is the input content, not the visual concept: three perfectly curated sources already contain the answers. Show document identity, selected evidence and the unresolved decision. Do not add a fake live terminal, invented processing duration or an autonomous-action badge.

**Reader.** Desktop chapter navigation is clear and the investor/internal labels are visible at both chapter and page level. The document reads as an actual artifact, which is valuable. Repeated instructional language (“Approved numbers. One consistent basis”, “What belongs with the figure”) makes pages 2–3 more like a product explainer than a believable investor report. Move explanatory teaching into the surrounding review annotation; let the sample use ordinary report headings. Keep basis/date/footer disclosures.

**Workflow accordion.** This repeats the workbench at lower evidentiary strength. Its invented little bar chart reduces credibility after the detailed proof. Keep as a compact route chooser only if each row links to the corresponding substantive page or scene. Investor activity is the one additional workflow; it needs a real follow-up example to merit equal prominence.

**Human approval section.** Its restraint is appropriate. Make it answer what the reviewer actually does: confirm the return basis, resolve unsupported answers, approve commentary. The existing large “C” is brand decoration; it is not proof of a control. Do not claim permissions, signatures or release prevention unless independently verified in production.

**Contact/footer.** A visible email and low-friction introduction are good. The next step needs a defined output, not another broad “what do you do manually?” question. Footer/sector links are adequate. There is no reason to introduce a long questionnaire, a mandatory upload of sensitive records, or unsupported security badges to this page.

## Strongest implementable fictional proof narrative

**“September is approved. Three things still need the fund team's attention.”** This is an authored review exercise using Anamorphic Capital, not a claim that an autonomous agent performed these actions. It should explain a workflow that can then be tested against the real product.

1. **A return was revised before the letter was finished.** Provide two small fictional administrator extracts: 3 October preliminary Class A USD September +1.90%, and 5 October approved Class A USD +2.10%. Provide PM notes separately, with their own approval/date. Show the approved version selected and the preliminary value explicitly superseded. The investor draft uses +2.10%, the existing correct +2.91% illustrative quarterly compound, and only sourced commentary. A nearby review note explains the version choice. This demonstrates avoiding a stale figure, not generating investment insight. Do not claim automated version resolution as shipped capability until verified.
2. **A cash explanation is plausible but unproved.** Provide separate PB and administrator extracts at the same specified cutoff and basis. Keep the existing 410,000 versus 535,000 balances and the operations note about an expected 125,000 receipt. Show the subtraction, a suggested follow-up to operations, and the unchanged open status. A user-controlled “Inspect evidence” opens the extracts and note; it does not trigger a fake request or mark the item resolved. A second optional authored state may show a later confirmation and proposed resolution, but only if its dates and balances actually support it and a human review remains explicit.
3. **An allocator question cannot be finished from last year's evidence.** Provide the original 5 October request, August policy extracts and the November 2025 test summary as distinct records. Show two draft answers with exact policy-section citations and one unanswered 2026 question. For NAV approval, either provide an explicit source or flag the approval authority as another gap. This demonstrates bounded drafting and evidence selection, not control effectiveness or a completed diligence process.

The output is visibly split into **Investor draft** (letter/performance/exposure) and **Internal review** (cash follow-up, flow schedule, allocator draft). An evidence strip shows the selected records and their dates. Each highlighted number/sentence opens the relevant excerpt rather than an omnibus workbook. The entire exercise can be implemented in the existing plain HTML/JS reader using static authored content; no new dependency or simulated backend is necessary.

If adding preliminary figures would overcomplicate the first iteration, retain only a clearly identified approved return and a separate PM note, but still split the source ownership. The priority is to make the work legible, not manufacture drama. Keep the fictional/prewritten disclosure beside the proof and on every sample artifact.

## Acceptance criteria for the next revision

- A five-second visitor can identify the fund team's remaining job and can enter the source/output proof directly.
- Every sample statement is either sourced, visibly calculated, or explicitly a proposed action; none implies an action already taken without a supporting record.
- A reviewer can distinguish class performance, fund NAV movement, dated exposure and later cash balances without relying on distant footnotes.
- The sample demonstrates at least one meaningful selection across distinct records; it does not start with a file that has already done all the synthesis.
- An investor-facing output is visibly separate from named flows and internal follow-ups, including its citation view.
- Cash matching never becomes full reconciliation; policy description never becomes proof of operating effectiveness; missing evidence never becomes a claim an event did not occur.
- The typewriter, established typefaces and paper identity remain. Charts contain labeled sample data or are replaced with actual document content.
- The CTA scopes one concrete workflow without inventing savings, customer proof, security assurances, delivery promises or production features.

The best next implementation is the distinct-source proof narrative plus the small traceability corrections. A prettier letter alone will not answer the buyer's comparison with tools and services they already use.
